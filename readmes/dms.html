Distribution Network

A web app for a distributor and the resellers who sell its stock. The distributor gets the admin side, with the product catalog, who's holding how much of what, and every sale. Each reseller logs into their own portal and only ever sees their own stock and their own sales.

There's a live deploy at dms.mahfujmustafa.dev, but everything is behind a login.

Built with Next.js 16, TypeScript, PostgreSQL and Prisma.

How it works

There are two roles, ADMIN for the distributor and RESELLER. The middleware reads the session token and sends each role to its own half of the app, so a reseller who types /admin into the address bar just lands back on their dashboard. The API routes check the role again on their own, and the reseller endpoints always filter by the logged-in user's id, so a reseller can't ask the server for someone else's numbers either.

Inventory is tracked per reseller. When the distributor sends units out, pulls them back or corrects a count, the change is saved as a stock movement (IN, OUT or ADJUSTMENT) in the same database transaction that updates the inventory row. That way the history and the counts can't drift apart. An OUT that would take a reseller below zero gets refused.

When a reseller logs a sale, the units come out of their inventory right away and the sale waits as pending until the distributor approves it. Only approved sales count toward revenue. Deleting a sale puts the units back.

Every product has a base cost and sell price, and it can also have bulk pricing tiers. A tier is a quantity with its own total price, like 10 units for one price and 50 for another, and the product form shows what each tier works out to per unit. When a sale is entered, the price fills in from the tier that matches the quantity. You can switch to a custom per-unit price or a flat total for the whole order if the deal was different.

The distributor dashboard shows total revenue and profit (sell price minus cost, on approved sales only), revenue for the last 30 days, how many sales are waiting for approval, a low-stock list for anything at 10 units or fewer, the top five products and resellers by revenue, and the latest sales and stock movements. The reports page breaks the same numbers down by day, category, reseller and product, including markup per product, over the last 7, 30 or 90 days, all time, or a custom date range.

Resellers get their own dashboard with their stock, their approved revenue, and what's still pending. The distributor can also switch into any reseller's account to see exactly what they see, then switch back.

Stack

Next.js 16 with the App Router and React 19, TypeScript, PostgreSQL through Prisma 7, NextAuth v5 with email and password login (passwords hashed with bcrypt) and JWT sessions, Tailwind CSS 4 with shadcn/ui components, Recharts for the charts, and Vercel Blob for product images.

Most of the business logic lives in src/lib/services/, and the API routes in src/app/api/ mostly just check who's asking and call into a service. Distributor pages are in src/app/admin/ and reseller pages are in src/app/reseller/.

Running it locally

You need Node 20.19+ or 22.12+ (Prisma 7's minimum) and a PostgreSQL database.

npm install

The postinstall script runs prisma generate, which writes the Prisma client to src/generated/prisma.

Create a .env file in the project root with these three values.

DATABASE_URL="postgresql://user:password@localhost:5432/dms"
AUTH_SECRET="a long random string"
BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN="your Vercel Blob token"

AUTH_SECRET signs the session tokens, and openssl rand -base64 32 will make you one. The Blob token is only used when you upload a product image, so you can leave it empty if you don't need thumbnails.

Then apply the migrations, seed the database and start the dev server.

npx prisma migrate dev
npx prisma db seed
npm run dev

The seed creates a single admin account, and its login is in prisma/seed.ts. Resellers are created from the admin side once you're in.

The other scripts are npm run build (generates the Prisma client, then builds), npm start and npm run lint.
